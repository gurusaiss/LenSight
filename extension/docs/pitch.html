The private AI layer for your browser.

Lensight lets you ask questions about whatever you're looking at — a page, a PDF, a selection — with your content indexed on your own device and sent to no one unless you choose to.

01 — Problem

Copy-paste is the real AI workflow today, and it's a bad one.

Every mainstream AI browser assistant asks you to trust its cloud with whatever you're reading — including the content you most want to keep private.

The workflow is broken

Select text, open a chatbot tab, paste, ask, repeat for every new page. It loses headings, timestamps, who-said-what — and has to happen again and again.

Every assistant is cloud-first

ChatGPT's browsing, Perplexity's Comet, Copilot in Edge, Arc — all send page content to a cloud model. Gemini in Chrome does a few tasks on-device, but it's closed and not configurable.

Private content is what you want to ask about

Messages, internal docs, dashboards — exactly what you should hesitate to paste into a consumer chatbot. Today you choose between convenience and privacy.

02 — Solution

Index it locally. Ask it anything. Choose where the answer comes from.

A Chrome extension that captures a page, selection, PDF, or file; indexes it on-device; answers with citations; and only leaves your machine if you explicitly pick a remote model.

03 — Why now

The category is forming, and no one owns local-first.

AI browser assistants are going mainstream

Copilot in Edge, Gemini in Chrome, Perplexity's own browser — users are learning to expect "ask about this page."

Every one of them is tied to a vendor's cloud

Most are tied to a vendor's browser too. None is local-first, privacy-first, and provider-agnostic at once — that's an open gap, not an incremental one.

Local AI has become practical

On-device embeddings run in-browser via WebAssembly; local LLM runtimes like Ollama run capable models on a user's own machine. Lensight uses both today without needing to train its own model.

Privacy expectations are rising

Manifest V3 and store policy push extensions toward least privilege. Lensight requests activeTab, not every site; runs no remote code; collects no telemetry.

06 — Business model

Designed for tiers. Not yet built. No revenue today.

The provider abstraction and the Session/Source data model already support usage limits — tiers are meant to be configuration, not a rewrite.

TierIntended offer
FreeFully local mode and basic remote-provider use, fair-use capped. Bring-your-own-key is always unlimited.
ProLarger local knowledge base, advanced automations, multi-tab intelligence, a bundled maintained provider.
TeamShared sessions and knowledge, opt-in sharing, enterprise-style controls.

Cost today is near zero — free/open tooling, no backend, users pay their own provider or nothing in local mode. Bundled-provider and team tiers introduce hosting and inference cost that needs modeling before launch.

07 — Traction

No users yet. Unusually rigorous engineering instead.

Pre-launch, honestly: no users, no revenue, no store listing. Built by one technical founder with AI-assisted engineering. What exists instead is a record of finding and fixing its own privacy and security bugs before anyone else could.

Findings included: files silently indexed as [object Object] on Chrome · a path where "fully local" mode could leak to a remote URL · a source-scope filter that quietly searched everything · prompt-injection escapes via Unicode and homoglyph tricks, now covered by 19 adversarial tests · a manifest permission that contradicted the privacy docs.

The point for investors — the hardest part of a privacy product is being trustworthy. Finding and fixing privacy bugs before launch, not after a breach, is the evidence.

10 — Risks, stated plainly

What could go wrong, and what's already done about it.

WhatsApp Web Terms of Service

WhatsApp's terms prohibit automated or bulk access, and its DOM changes without notice. Mitigation: one experimental adapter, not the flagship — user-triggered, one conversation at a time, on-screen content only, disclosed to users.

Free AI provider tiers may change

Mode B convenience depends on providers' free tiers and terms. Mitigation: any OpenAI-compatible endpoint works, users can bring their own key, and fully local mode needs no provider at all.

Chrome Web Store review

Extensions reading page content, shipping WASM, and calling AI APIs get scrutiny. Mitigation: least-privilege manifest, no remote code, no eval, zero production advisories, a written privacy policy. Still unproven until submitted.

"Local-first" is a harder sell

"Just use our cloud AI" is simpler to explain. Zero-setup local mode returns ranked excerpts, not fluent prose; real local generation needs Ollama installed. Mitigation: lead with technical and privacy-sensitive users; the desktop companion is the intended path to simpler local AI.

Platform competition

Google, Microsoft and others could add a local mode. Mitigation: their incentive is their own cloud and browser — Lensight is cross-provider and privacy-built from the start.

Unverified pieces

Real-model embedding quality, live Ollama/remote-API calls, the live WhatsApp adapter, and the desktop GUI haven't been exercised end to end yet. Documented explicitly, and first on the launch checklist.
